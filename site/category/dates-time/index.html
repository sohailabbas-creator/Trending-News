Dates are not arithmetic

Adding 30 days is not the same as adding one month. A notice period starting on 31 January has no 31 February to land on, which is why legal counting rules exist and why the inclusive/exclusive choice changes the answer by a day. The date duration calculator gives the result in years, months and days as well as in raw days and weeks, and it lets you pick the counting convention instead of guessing.

Time zones are rules, not offsets

A fixed offset such as UTC+1 is not a time zone. Europe/London switches between GMT and BST, and the transition dates differ from those in the United States, so the London–New York gap is five hours for most of the year and four hours for a couple of weeks in spring and autumn. The time zone converter uses the browser's IANA database, so the answer is correct for the specific date you select — including those awkward weeks.

Age is a legal question as well as a personal one

Age in completed years on a specific date decides eligibility for exams, visas, pensions and school entry. The age calculator uses the years-months-days method that forms ask for, handles 29 February birthdays explicitly, and can measure age on any future date rather than only today.
