What the Loan EMI Calculator does

An equated monthly instalment keeps the payment the same every month while the split between interest and principal shifts. Interest is charged on the outstanding balance, so in the early years most of each payment is interest; by the final years almost all of it is principal. That is why a 20-year loan at 9% costs almost twice its face value in interest, and why prepaying in year three saves far more than prepaying in year fifteen.

This calculator applies the standard reducing-balance formula, shows the full amortisation schedule, and compares your result against a scenario where you add a fixed extra amount to each payment, so you can see the interest saved and the months cut.

How to use it

  1. Enter the loan amount, the annual interest rate and the term in years or months.
  2. Add any processing fee if you want it included in the effective cost.
  3. Optionally enter an extra monthly payment to see its effect.
  4. Read the EMI, total interest and total payable, then scroll the schedule to see how the balance falls.

Features

  • Standard reducing-balance EMI formula, shown on screen
  • Total interest and total payable alongside the monthly payment
  • Month-by-month amortisation with principal and interest split
  • Extra-payment comparison showing interest saved and months saved
  • Handles zero-interest loans and terms entered in months or years

Worked examples

250,000 over 20 years at 9.5%

The EMI is about 2,330 a month. Total repayment is about 559,300, so interest alone is roughly 309,300 — more than the amount borrowed. In the first year, interest accounts for about 92% of what you pay.

The same loan with 200 extra each month

Paying 200 more every month clears the balance around five years earlier and saves roughly 90,000 in interest. The earlier the extra payment starts, the larger the saving, because it attacks the balance that interest is charged on.

Frequently asked questions

How is EMI calculated?

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of monthly payments. The result stays constant while the split between interest and principal changes each month.

What is the difference between flat rate and reducing balance interest?

Flat rate charges interest on the original amount for the whole term, so a 10% flat rate on 100,000 over five years costs 50,000 in interest. Reducing balance charges interest only on what is still owed, so the same nominal 10% costs roughly 27,000. Always compare APRs, not headline rates.

Does prepaying a loan save interest?

Yes, and the saving is largest early in the term because the outstanding balance is highest. Check whether your lender charges a prepayment penalty and whether the loan uses reducing balance; on a flat-rate loan the saving is calculated differently.

Why is my bank's EMI slightly different?

Differences usually come from fees rolled into the principal, a different day-count convention, rounding to the nearest rupee, pound or euro each month, or an insurance product added to the loan.

Is a longer tenure always cheaper per month?

It lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest. Stretching a 100,000 loan from 10 to 20 years at 9.5% cuts the payment from about 1,294 to 932 but roughly doubles the interest paid.
