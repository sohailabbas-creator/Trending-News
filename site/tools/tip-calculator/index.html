What the Tip Calculator does

Tipping is a percentage calculation with a cultural variable on top. In the United States 18% to 20% at a sit-down restaurant is customary and often expected; in the UK a discretionary 12.5% service charge is frequently already on the bill; in much of Germany, the Netherlands and Spain rounding up or leaving 5% to 10% is normal; in Japan tipping can be considered impolite.

This calculator handles the arithmetic — tip on the pre-tax or post-tax amount, then an even split between any number of people — and the guide below stops you double-tipping a service charge that is already included.

How to use it

  1. Enter the bill amount.
  2. Choose whether the bill already includes tax, and add the tax rate if you want the tip calculated on the pre-tax amount.
  3. Set the tip percentage, or use a preset, then choose how many people are paying.
  4. Read the tip, the total and the per-person share, rounded to a sensible amount.

Frequently asked questions

Should I tip on the pre-tax or post-tax amount?

Most guidance suggests tipping on the pre-tax subtotal, since tax is not part of the service. Many people tip on the total for simplicity; the difference on a typical bill is small. Either way, do not tip on a service charge that is already included.

How much should I tip for delivery?

In the US, 15% to 20% of the order, with a minimum of a few dollars, is common — delivery drivers often rely on tips and may use their own vehicle. In Europe, 5% to 10% or a rounding-up is usually enough.

Is a service charge the same as a tip?

Not always. A mandatory service charge is usually distributed to staff by the employer, sometimes pooled, and in some jurisdictions it is legally treated as part of the employer's revenue. A voluntary tip given directly is more certain to reach the person who served you.

How do I split a bill when people ordered differently?

This tool splits evenly. For uneven bills, calculate each person's share of the food and drink, then apply the same tip percentage to each share — that keeps the tip proportional without needing a separate calculation.
