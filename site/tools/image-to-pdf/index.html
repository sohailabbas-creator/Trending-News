    What the Image to PDF does

    Turning photos into a PDF is the fastest way to send a document when you do not have a scanner: photograph each page, then combine them into one file in reading order. The usual online converters upload every photo, add a watermark or cap you at three files.

    This converter builds the PDF locally. Choose A4 or Letter, portrait or landscape, and whether the image should fill the page or fit inside the margins, then download a single multi-page PDF with no watermark and no file limit beyond your device's memory.

    How to use it

    1. Select the images in the order you want the pages to appear.
    2. Choose page size: A4 or Letter, and portrait or landscape.
    3. Set the margin and whether images are fitted or cropped to fill the page.
    4. Press Create PDF and download the file.

    Features

    • Multi-page PDF from any number of images
    • A4 and Letter page sizes, portrait or landscape
    • Fit or fill modes with adjustable margins
    • Reorder before converting
    • No upload, no watermark, no page limit

    Worked examples

    A four-page signed contract

    Four phone photos become one A4 PDF in reading order, around 1.5 MB at typical phone resolution. Fitting rather than filling keeps the whole page visible, with white margins instead of cropped edges.

    Receipts for expenses

    Ten receipts, one page each, in date order, makes an expense claim that finance can approve without opening ten separate images.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is there a watermark?

    No. The PDF is generated locally with no branding, no footer and no trial limitation.

    Are my photos uploaded?

    No. Images are read from disk into browser memory and embedded in a PDF you download. Nothing is sent over the network.

    Should I use A4 or Letter?

    A4 for Europe, the UK and most of the world; Letter for the United States and Canada. If the recipient will print it, match their paper size rather than converting later.

    Why do my photos look cropped?

    Because 'fill' mode scales the image until it covers the page, trimming the edges. Switch to 'fit' to guarantee the entire photo is visible, which is the safer choice for documents and signatures.

    How large will the PDF be?

    Roughly the total size of the source images plus a small overhead. Compress the images first if the PDF is too large to email — a typical mailbox limit is 10 MB to 25 MB.
