Percentages: the sentence is the formula

Almost every percentage mistake comes from answering the wrong question. 'What is 15% of 80' is multiplication. '15 is what percent of 80' is division. 'A price rose from 80 to 92' is a change calculation on the original value. 'A price of 92 includes 15% tax' requires division by 1.15, not subtraction of 15%. The percentage calculator has a mode for each, and prints the formula it used so you can confirm which one you needed.

BMI: a screening number, not a diagnosis

Body mass index is a population-level screening tool. It is cheap, reproducible and useful across large groups, and it cannot distinguish muscle from fat. The calculator shows the WHO bands and the healthy weight range for your height so the number can be interpreted in context, and it is explicit that athletes, older adults, children and some ethnic groups need different measures or thresholds.

Use them together

Both tools are often the first step in a bigger question. A percentage result may feed a discount, a VAT calculation or a loan comparison; a BMI result may lead to a conversation with a clinician. The related tools on each page point to the next step.
