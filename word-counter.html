What the Word & Character Counter does

Word and character limits are everywhere: 1,000-word assignments, 220-character SMS limits, 60-character title tags, 500-character personal statements. Counting by hand is not realistic, and word processor counts differ because they treat hyphens, emoji and punctuation differently.

This counter updates as you type and shows words, characters with and without spaces, sentences, paragraphs, reading time and the most repeated meaningful words. Everything happens locally, so drafts, contracts and private notes are never uploaded.

How to use it

  1. Paste your text, or type directly in the box.
  2. Watch the counters update live above the text.
  3. Check the without-spaces figure when a form specifies it — many do.
  4. Use the keyword panel to see which terms you are repeating most.

Features

  • Words, characters with and without spaces, sentences and paragraphs
  • Reading time and speaking time estimates
  • Top keyword density, ignoring common stop words
  • Average word length and unique word count
  • Copy a summary of all stats in one click

Worked examples

500 words in characters

At an average English word length of about 5.1 characters plus a space, 500 words is roughly 3,000 characters with spaces and 2,550 without. Use the live counter for the exact figure rather than the estimate.

Fit a meta description

Google typically truncates descriptions beyond about 155–160 characters. Write the sentence, then check the character count here and trim until it fits without cutting a word in half.

Frequently asked questions

Does the counter include spaces in the character count?

Both figures are shown. Characters with spaces counts every keystroke including spaces and line breaks; characters without spaces excludes whitespace. Forms usually specify which one they mean — if not, assume with spaces.

How is reading time calculated?

Words divided by 238 words per minute, the widely cited average silent reading speed for adults. Speaking time uses 130 words per minute. Both are rounded to the nearest sensible unit and are estimates, not promises.

How does my word processor get a different count?

Word processors disagree on hyphenated words, abbreviations, numbers, emoji and trailing punctuation. Microsoft Word counts a hyphenated compound as one word; some tools count it as two. If a limit is strict, check which convention the recipient uses.

Is my text uploaded anywhere?

No. Counting runs in JavaScript inside your browser and the text is discarded when you close the tab or clear the box.
