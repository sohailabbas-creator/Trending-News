Limits, and which one applies

Forms specify character limits, sometimes with spaces and sometimes without, and word processors disagree about hyphens and numbers. The word counter shows both character figures at once so you do not have to guess, along with reading time and the words you are repeating most.

Case is a formatting decision with consequences

Title case has rules, not just capital letters — short prepositions stay lowercase unless they open the title. Developer naming conventions are stricter still: kebab-case for URLs and CSS because search engines treat hyphens as separators, snake_case for JSON keys and database columns, camelCase for JavaScript variables. The case converter handles all of them and preserves line breaks, so a whole document converts at once.

Diffs and JSON catch what eyes miss

Human attention follows meaning, so a deleted 'not' can pass a proofread. A line-based diff marks every change in red and green and counts them. JSON has no comments and no tolerance: one trailing comma breaks the whole document, which is why the formatter reports the exact line and character rather than a generic parse error.
