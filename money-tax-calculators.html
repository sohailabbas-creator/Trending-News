Why these calculators show the formula

Financial mistakes are rarely arithmetic mistakes — they are direction mistakes. Adding VAT and removing VAT need different operations. Percentage increase and percentage decrease do not cancel out. Overtime is paid on a base rate that may or may not include shift premiums. When the formula is on the page, you can see which direction the tool went and catch a wrong assumption before it reaches an invoice or a payslip.

What these tools do not do

None of them estimate tax you owe. Income tax, national insurance, social security and pension rules depend on where you live, your household and your employment status, and a generic deduction would be wrong for most visitors. The salary calculator converts gross pay between periods and says so; the VAT calculator applies the rate you choose and tells you to confirm that rate with your tax authority. For anything filed with a government, use the official calculator.

Where to start

If you are invoicing, start with the VAT calculator. If you are comparing a job offer or a contract, start with the salary calculator and then check overtime. If you are borrowing, the loan calculator's amortisation schedule shows how much of an early repayment goes to principal, which is the number that actually decides whether prepaying is worth it.
