What the Percentage Calculator does

A percentage is just a fraction with a denominator of 100, but the phrase around it changes the arithmetic completely. 20% of 150 is 30. 30 as a percentage of 150 is 20%. A move from 150 to 180 is a 20% increase, while 180 back down to 150 is a 16.7% decrease. Each of these is a different question, which is why so many people reach for a calculator rather than risk the wrong formula.

This tool has four modes covering those cases, shows the formula it used, and includes the reverse case — working backwards from a final amount to the original — which is the one most people get wrong when removing a tax, a discount or a fee.

How to use it

  1. Pick the mode that matches your sentence: percent of a number, percent of one number from another, change between two numbers, or reverse percentage.
  2. Type the two values.
  3. Read the answer together with the formula, so you can check it against your own working.
  4. Switch modes to solve a second part of the same question without reloading.

Worked examples

Test score

You scored 68 out of 85. 68 ÷ 85 × 100 = 80%. If the pass mark is 75% you passed, but as a share of the 100 available marks your result is 68%, not 80% — the denominator matters.

Removing a 15% service charge

A bill of 138 includes a 15% charge. The original is 138 ÷ 1.15 = 120, and the charge is 18. Taking 15% off 138 gives 117.30, which is 2.70 too low — a good reminder that removing a percentage means dividing, not subtracting.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between percentage change and percentage points?

Percentage points measure the simple difference between two percentages: a rate moving from 5% to 8% rises 3 percentage points. Percentage change measures that move relative to the starting value: the same jump is a 60% increase. News stories about interest rates usually mean percentage points.

How do I calculate a reverse percentage?

If you know the amount after a percentage has been added, divide by 1 plus the rate as a decimal. If it was removed, divide by 1 minus the rate. For 20% VAT, 600 becomes 500 because 600 ÷ 1.2 = 500.

Why do percentage increases and decreases not cancel out?

Because they apply to different bases. A 50% rise from 100 gives 150; a 50% fall from 150 gives 75. To return to the original value after a 50% rise you need a 33.3% fall.

Can percentages be negative?

Yes. A negative change simply means a decrease. This calculator shows the sign explicitly so a fall is never confused with a rise.
