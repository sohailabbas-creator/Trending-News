What the Time Zone Converter does

Converting a single time is easy until daylight saving is involved and one region shifts while another does not. New York and London are five hours apart for most of the year, but four hours for the two weeks in March and the week in late October when the transition dates differ. That is exactly when meetings get missed.

This converter uses the IANA time zone database through your browser, so every offset and daylight-saving transition is handled for you. Pick a source city and time, and see the same moment in every city you care about, with working-hours highlighted so you can spot the realistic slots rather than the theoretical ones.

How to use it

  1. Choose the city you are converting from and the time you mean.
  2. Add the cities you need to compare — up to eight at once.
  3. Read the local time in each city, with daylight saving already applied.
  4. Look for rows marked as working hours when picking a slot for a call.

Features

  • Uses the IANA time zone database, so daylight saving is correct
  • Compare up to eight cities at once

  • Live 'now' mode plus any date you choose
  • Shows each city's UTC offset so you can sanity-check

Worked examples

London morning, New York morning

10:00 in London in winter is 05:00 in New York — outside working hours. 14:00 London is 09:00 New York, which is usually the earliest comfortable overlap for a call between the two.

The daylight saving trap

In the two weeks when US daylight saving has started but European summer time has not, the London–New York gap is four hours, not five. A recurring weekly call booked in that window will move by an hour for one side.

Frequently asked questions

How is daylight saving handled?

The browser's Intl API uses the IANA time zone database, so each city's offset is calculated for the specific date you choose, including transition dates. That is why the same pair of cities can be four hours apart in March and five hours apart in April.

What is the difference between a time zone and an offset?

A time zone is a region with a set of rules, such as Europe/London, which switches between GMT and BST. An offset is a fixed number such as UTC+1. Booking systems that ask for an offset instead of a zone are where recurring meetings usually break.

Why do some places not observe daylight saving?

Most of Asia, Africa and the tropics do not shift, because the daylight benefit is small near the equator. China uses a single zone across five geographic zones. Japan and India do not observe daylight saving.

How do I find a meeting time that works for everyone?

Start with the city that has the most constrained working hours, usually the furthest east or west, then slide the source time until that city is inside 09:00–17:00. If no overlap exists at all, an asynchronous update or a rotating meeting time is more respectful than a 23:00 call.
