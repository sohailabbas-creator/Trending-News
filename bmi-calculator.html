What the BMI Calculator does

Body mass index divides weight in kilograms by height in metres squared. It was designed as a population-level screening tool in the 19th century, and it is still used because it is cheap, reproducible and correlates roughly with body fat across large groups. What it cannot do is tell muscle from fat, which is why a muscular athlete can score as overweight while carrying very little body fat.

This calculator gives your BMI, your category on the World Health Organization adult scale and the weight range that would place you in the healthy band for your height. Read it as a starting point for a conversation, not a diagnosis.

How to use it

  1. Choose metric (cm and kg) or imperial (feet, inches and pounds).
  2. Enter your height and weight.
  3. Read your BMI and its WHO category.
  4. Check the healthy weight range shown for your height to see where the boundaries fall.

Features

  • Shows the boundaries in the same units you entered

Worked examples

175 cm and 72 kg

72 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 23.5, which is inside the healthy 18.5–24.9 band. The healthy weight range for 175 cm is roughly 56.6 kg to 76.3 kg.

A muscular 180 cm athlete at 95 kg

BMI is 29.3, which reads as overweight. If body fat is 12%, the category is misleading. Waist-to-height ratio and body composition tests are more useful in this case.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI?

The World Health Organization puts the healthy adult range at 18.5 to 24.9. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25 to 29.9 is overweight and 30 or above is obese. The bands are the same for men and women, but not for children.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

No. BMI cannot distinguish muscle from fat, so strength athletes and rugby players routinely score as overweight or obese while carrying low body fat. Waist-to-height ratio and a body composition measurement are better in that situation.

Does BMI work for children?

Not with adult bands. Children and teenagers need age- and sex-specific percentile charts, because body composition changes rapidly during growth. Ask a paediatrician which chart applies.

Do ethnicity and age change BMI interpretation?

Yes. Some health bodies use lower thresholds for people of South Asian, Chinese, Japanese and other Asian descent, where type 2 diabetes risk rises at a lower BMI. Older adults also carry more fat at the same BMI, so clinicians often combine BMI with waist circumference.

How can I lower my BMI?

Gradual, sustainable changes work better than crash diets: a modest calorie deficit, more protein and fibre, strength training to preserve muscle, and 150 minutes of moderate activity a week. Speak to a doctor before starting a strict plan, especially with existing conditions.
